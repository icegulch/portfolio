## Leading the big data conversation (Gnip / Twitter)

In 2011, big data wasn't yet a buzzword — it was a genuine frontier, and Gnip was one of the companies helping define it. As a data firehose aggregator (think: the pipes behind Twitter, Facebook, and dozens of other social platforms), Gnip wanted to convene the people building the data-driven web — engineers, data scientists, product leaders — for an honest, practitioner-focused conversation about where things were headed.

Big Boulder Conference was that conversation — held annually in Boulder.

I led the digital and creative production for six years — from its inaugural edition through its 2018 wind-down — first at dojo4, then under With Brio when I went independent. When Twitter acquired Gnip in 2014, the conference continued. The Twitter team asked me to stick around to help the event grow.

## Four tools, one visual language

Big Boulder ran on four distinct web properties, each serving a different audience and built on a different platform:

- **Marketing site** — the public face of the conference, rebuilt annually with a fresh but consistent visual identity (static site generator, GitHub, Netlify)
- **Splash** — event registration, ticketing, scheduling, and the dynamic onsite session displays that helped attendees navigate the conference in real time
- **Tendenci** — a Django-based member management platform serving the broader Big Boulder community between annual events
- **MailChimp** — our chosen email marketing tool for keeping speakers, sponsors, attendees, and members informed across the conference lifecycle

The challenge wasn't building any one of them — it was making all four feel like the same conference despite running on completely different stacks, with different constraints, different interaction patterns, and different teams touching each one.

A consistent visual language — typography, color, iconography — became the connective tissue that held the experience together across platforms the attendee never thought about but always felt.

## Print, signage, and swag

Every year, Big Boulder also meant a full suite of print materials — programs, brochures, signage, and branded swag — all designed to feel like extensions of that year's visual identity, not afterthoughts.

Conference swag extended beyond the expected. For SXSW, we produced a branded whiskey flask (shown at the top of this page) — the kind of thing that actually makes it home from a conference bag, gets used, and keeps the brand alive long after the event ends.

Each year's print suite had to be designed, produced, and delivered under hard deadlines — vendor coordination, print specs, shipping logistics, and proofing all happening in parallel with the web and registration work. Across digital, print, and registration — no one piece existed in isolation. Everything shipped together or nothing worked.

When Twitter wound down the conference's registration services in 2018, that work fell to me too — by then, I knew where everything lived.

Six consecutive years of a recurring annual event, produced end to end, for one of the most recognizable names in tech. The kind of project that doesn't show up as a single deliverable — it shows up as a conference that simply *happened*, year after year, without anyone outside the team noticing the seams.
