## The idea

In 2015, I created an 18 × 24-inch “periodic table” of New Hampshire's 4,000-foot mountains as a kind of joke gift for a hiking friend. That friend saw it and said: “This is no joke! You _need_ to sell these!!”

Fast forward eleven years, with distribution through REI, The Mountaineer, the Appalachian Mountain Club, and other outdoor retailers, and I’ve sold over 30,000 products, including over 5,000 of those prints and 25,000 of the hiking passports (log books) they inspired.

This “side trail” into print design has resulted in some of the most compelling user testimonials I’ve ever received. And it's also allowed me to step out of the web domain and into the adjacent-but-very-different realm of print production.

## Borrowing proven paradigms

The instinct behind every product in the line is the same: take a format people already understand — a periodic table, a passport, a transit diagram — and use it to organize information in a novel way. The format does the work of making dense information legible at a glance.

Behind each product is structured data — names, categories, attributes. Adobe Illustrator's “variable data” feature is made for this, providing consistent formatting and content stability (single source of truth) alongside creative must-haves like the pen tool. Content modeling, applied to ink and paper.

## What's next?

Much of the information in these publications changes over time, so I make regular updates and face down ever-emerging challenges (e.g., will there be enough room for new stickers?). But, between edits and wholesale fulfillments, the creative juices are still flowing.

I have a few more ideas.
